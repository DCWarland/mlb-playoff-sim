Either side of a line moves both clubs, because a game one wins is a game the other loses. Overridden lines are shaded. Results read left club first.

Projected standings

Ordered by projected finish, and it re-sorts as you drag — clubs change places and the cut line moves with them. Division leaders are whoever is projected to lead, not whoever leads today. ±Cut is projected wins above or below the third wild card, so a positive number is a club currently forecast to play in October. Opp is the average strength of who a club has left. Projections are exact arithmetic rather than simulated, so they carry no lag; the odds column comes from the simulation and settles a moment behind.

The road in

A magic number is the oldest arithmetic in baseball: how many combined events must land your way before a result is guaranteed, whatever happens afterwards. Each of your own wins counts one. So does each loss by the club you are chasing. When it reaches zero it is settled. Nothing here is simulated or predicted — it is only games already played, so the sliders have no effect on this tab. It moves once a day, on real results.

Who to root for

Each game on the next day of the schedule, simulated both ways: once with the home club winning, once with the away club. The bar is how much your odds move between those two worlds, so the longest bar is the game that matters most tonight.

Every simulated season is seeded from its own number, so season 7 unfolds identically in both runs apart from this one result. That is what makes the comparison trustworthy — the difference is the effect of the game, not the luck of the draw. Sharing a single stream is not enough: tie-breaks and the playoff bracket draw a variable number of numbers and knock the two runs out of step, which is exactly how an earlier version of this chart ended up reporting noise.

The season so far

Every finished game is on record with its date, so the season can be replayed a day at a time. At each step the standings are rebuilt from the games played by then, the schedule still to come is whatever was dated later, and the odds are simulated from that — using only what was known at the time. The result is the shape of the season.

Scored against reality

The honest test, and it responds to the model sliders — change them under Model in the rail and watch this re-score. Walking the season in order, every finished game is scored against what the model would have said before it was played, using only the records as they stood that morning. If the model is calibrated, games it called 60% should have been won about 60% of the time. The line is what happened; the faint diagonal is perfection.
